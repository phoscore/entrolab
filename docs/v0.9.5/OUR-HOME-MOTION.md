# Our Home: relative motion and distance (Phase 2F-A)

The Solar System canvas is a view from north of the J2000 ecliptic plane, with the Sun at the origin. Nine quick choices select the Sun and the eight planets. Choose a target and a reference, then use **Jump to body**, **Follow selection**, or **Full system**. A dashed line connects the selected model positions only when both are in the viewport. It is a projected line, not the 3D distance. The comparison remains available in the inspector when zoom hides either endpoint.

At one model epoch in TDB, both positions come from the existing JPL Table 1 approximation, in the same heliocentric J2000 ecliptic frame. For positions **r** and model velocities **v**, the comparison uses **r** = **r**ₐ − **r**ᵦ, distance = |**r**|, **v**rel = **v**ₐ − **v**ᵦ, speed = |**v**rel| and signed radial speed = **r** · **v**rel / |**r**|. Positive radial speed means the separation is increasing. The map separation is √(rₓ² + rᵧ²); the displayed spatial distance includes z. AU is exactly 149,597,870.7 km; the one-way illustrative light time is distance / 299,792.458 km/s with the geometry frozen at that epoch. This is not an iterative light-time solution or a prediction of a received signal.

Model velocities are derived from the *unchanged* approximate positions using a symmetric difference at ±0.01 TDB day (14.4 minutes), with second-order one-sided differences within one step of 1800-01-01 or 2050-01-01. The support is 1800-01-01 00:00 to 2050-01-01 00:00 TDB, inclusive; samples never extrapolate beyond it. Truncation error is second order in the step and depends on curvature; floating-point subtraction limits extremely small steps. An independent five-point derivative with ±0.02 day (four-point at both endpoints) is compared at six epochs and five bodies in `tests/solar-systems.test.mjs`, with a 0.0001 km/s tolerance. The observed maximum discrepancy in the initial local check was 0.0000144 km/s. This is a numerical consistency check **within the approximation**, not validation against a high-precision ephemeris. The JPL fit's nominal positional errors and neglected perturbations dominate any such numerical agreement; values are deliberately presented with approximation marks and four significant digits.

The existing `earth` model position is the **Earth–Moon barycentre**, not Earth's geocentre. In a comparison, the UI spells out Earth–Moon barycentre in both languages. The Sun's heliocentric velocity is zero *by definition of this frame*, not an absolute velocity through the Galaxy. The model offers no measured instantaneous state vectors, moons, Pluto, orbital trails, or galactic velocity. [JPL orbital model and stated accuracy](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html).

## Gaia overview raster: P3 correction

The separate first commit feathers alpha inward on the occupied side of counted cells. Empty cells retain alpha zero, and source counts, filters, tile data, hit testing, and all release hashes remain unchanged. The synthetic edge test and real 128×128 density arrays for all three Gaia filters check that every occupied centre stays visible and every empty centre transparent. Screenshots at 1440 and 320 px in the browser evidence compare the frozen PR #28 baseline with this branch, Gaia alone and Gaia with DESI. Smoothing only the occupied side leaves sharp boundaries between a positive and a zero cell where necessary to avoid depicting an unobserved source.
