# Our Home · Free camera

Open any local 3D scene and choose **Free camera** beside the view presets. **Orbit** switches back. The starting camera is Orbit. In free mode drag the map or press the arrow keys to look. With the map focused, hold W/S to move along the view, A/D to strafe and Q/E to move down/up along the camera basis. The six on-screen direction buttons accept continuous mouse or touch holds. Focus one and press Enter or Space for a short step. Tab or Escape leaves map input. No pointer lock is acquired. The speed slider uses 17 settings, each a factor of approximately 3.16 from its neighbour. The selected speed setting carries between scenes and each scene interprets it at its own spatial scale. Values are relative to real time and describe movement of the observer, independent of the simulation clock.

## Camera state and projection

Coordinates are JavaScript Float64 AU values in the scene's existing displayed reference frame. Given the orbit focus **F**, outward unit vector **T** and focus distance **D** = base distance / 10^zoom, the free start position is **P** = **F** + **D T**. Yaw and pitch are preserved. The camera basis is the existing orthonormal right/up/toward basis. The view looks along −**T**. Free projection subtracts **P** from each world vector, then uses the same perspective transform. World vectors, ephemerides and numerical inspectors are independent of the camera.

Return uses the forward point at the last orbit depth. If the scene's base distance changed, the depth is clamped to the supported Orbit zoom range and the point is recomputed from the same eye position and yaw/pitch. There is no automatic object snap. The perspective matches on either side for common visible geometry. The near plane can differ. In free mode it is 2.5% of the reference depth clamped to [10⁻¹⁰, 10⁻³] AU. Segment clipping uses the same near-plane routine as Orbit. Translation clamps each component to ±10⁶ AU. Per-frame elapsed time is capped at 80 ms to avoid jumps after stalls. Combined directions are normalized, and rendered glyph radii are capped in close views. The focus-depth scale bar is hidden in Free camera. The displayed speed is the selected local movement rate at the current camera scale, rather than a physical velocity. The rate is 0.16 × reference depth per real second at the middle setting, with global bounds [10⁻¹¹, 10⁴] AU per real second.

## Scenes, navigation and lifecycle

- **Solar System and small bodies** use heliocentric J2000-ecliptic view coordinates. **Moon systems** reuse each existing local origin, with positions converted from km into AU before projection.
- **Voyager and Heliosphere** use the current heliocentric scene. Crossover controls retain the previous scene's camera on return. **Galactic Motion** uses its existing local galactic display coordinates, including the explicitly labelled schematic mode.
- A fresh subview opens in Orbit with its existing fit and clock rules. Return restores the saved camera, including free position, unless switching to 2D. Explicit fit, object centre and flyby jumps work in both camera types. Fit repositions the free camera from the fitted Orbit pose. Automatic orbit following is suspended on entering Free.
- Drag, direction buttons, speed changes and camera toggles do not animate any transition. Held inputs are cleared on release, capture loss, focus loss, tab visibility change and mode exit. The existing render callback runs only while something moves or a redraw is needed. Reduced Motion continues to suppress automatic playback under the existing rules.

The pure camera maths and pointer/key input module are separate from the canvas projection and orbit/data code. The renderer still uses perspective Canvas and painter ordering. Deep nested scales, hardware depth buffering and large-catalogue level of detail are later architecture work. Distances and orientation may be hard to read far from all scene objects. Use the existing Fit button or center a selected object to return. The same math has no dependency on the particular planetary catalogue and can be reused by a later renderer.

The Node suite covers orthogonality, three-axis displacement, look independence, normalization, timing, near clipping and transitions. Native Chromium validates actual three-coordinate movement, mouse drag without translation, touch at 390/320 pixels, view restoration, localization and 100 repeated switches.
