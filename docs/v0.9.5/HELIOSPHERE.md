# Our Home · Heliosphere

Open **Cosmic Explorer → Our Solar System → Heliosphere · 3D**. The first view frames the schematic surfaces and spacecraft. Rotate, zoom, choose a region or centre a probe. The **Observed Voyager crossings** section jumps to historical events. Closing the layer restores the previous view, including its date, camera and selections. The map uses the local Sun-centred frame. From Galactic Motion, select Sun-centred to open the layer. As in the established Voyager and moon modes, switching views pauses playback. The heliosphere surfaces are static at every time. Model time updates the planets and trajectories.

## Physical interpretation and evidence

The solar wind is a magnetized plasma outflow. Its interaction with the partially ionized local interstellar medium forms the heliosphere. At the termination shock, the outward wind is abruptly slowed and compressed. Energy is redistributed among the plasma, fields and energetic particles, including pick-up ions. The heliosheath contains this processed solar wind and extends to the heliopause, a plasma boundary. Neutral atoms participate in charge exchange and can cross it. The flow is diverted downstream into the heliotail. Solar-wind pressure changes, interstellar flow and the magnetic environment give the boundaries an asymmetric, time-dependent shape. Voyager samples along two paths at different epochs, so crossing-distance differences mix direction and time. [Voyager review](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11214-022-00899-y) · [NASA shape overview](https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/uncovering-our-solar-systems-shape/).

IBEX and IMAP measure energetic neutral atoms (ENAs), whose emission carries information about the source plasma along each line of sight. Travel times, reaction rates and visibility enter the interpretation. A pressure transient and its delayed ENA response let [Zirnstein et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-022-01798-6) infer oblique, irregular and variable boundary structures in the mapped directions. [IBEX data releases](https://ibex.princeton.edu/DataRelease) include solar-cycle products. [Opher et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-020-1036-0) model a more compact heliosphere by treating pick-up ions as a separate fluid. A long comet-like tail and compact or lobed model geometries depend on the treatment of charge exchange, fields, plasma and boundary conditions. ENA maps and in-situ trajectories constrain these models without uniquely reconstructing a global surface.

IMAP launched on 24 September 2025, published preliminary ENA first-light maps, and began its primary science mission on 1 February 2026. NASA announced a first public science release for seven instruments on 7 August 2026. The three ENA instruments were outside that release. Their mapping capability addresses the shape and variability questions. Neither the commissioning maps nor that release is used to prescribe a surface in this app. [First light](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/imap/2025/12/16/nasas-imap-mission-captures-first-light-looks-back-at-earth/) · [Mission](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/imap/) · [August release](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/imap/2026/08/07/nasas-imap-mission-releases-new-data-from-7-science-instruments/).

The planetary system, Kuiper Belt, heliosphere and hypothesised Oort Cloud describe different populations or physical domains. The Kuiper Belt lies beyond Neptune and consists of small icy bodies. The Oort Cloud is a much more distant inferred reservoir of comets. The Sun still gravitates outside the heliopause. Voyager's crossing means entering interstellar plasma in the local neighbourhood. [NASA Voyager Interstellar Mission](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/interstellar-mission/) · [NASA Kuiper Belt](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/kuiper-belt/) · [NASA Oort Cloud](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/oort-cloud/).

## Schematic world geometry

Sun at (0,0,0). All lengths are AU using 149,597,870.7 km/AU. X is the J2000 ecliptic reference longitude, Z the north ecliptic pole and Y completes a right-handed frame. The schematic upwind axis uses J2000 ecliptic longitude 256°, latitude +5°, a rounded direction **toward** the incoming neutral helium in the six-year [IBEX inflow fit](https://wurz.space.unibe.ch/Bzowsky_ApJS_2015.pdf). This is an orientation reference. The interstellar magnetic field, asymmetry and detailed shape are not inferred from that direction.

Use orthonormal vectors along the nose, east (increasing longitude) and north (increasing latitude). For cosine `c` of the polar angle from the nose and azimuth `φ` from east toward north, let `s = sqrt(1 − c²)`, `u = s sin φ` and `d = max(0, −c)`. Each radial shell uses the direction `c·nose + s cos φ·east + u·north`.

```text
R_heliopause = 126 − 12 c + 135 d² + 9 u + 7 s² cos(2 φ)  [AU]
R_shock      = R_heliopause · (0.73 − 0.12 d + 0.04 u)    [AU]
```

The numbers are illustrative parameters with upwind scales guided by Voyager. The nominal nose radii are about 114 AU and 83 AU. The long axis expands smoothly downstream. The finite rear cap closes the drawing and specifies no measured extent or particular tail topology. The latitude term tilts the radial balance and the azimuth term varies widths. The positive radial functions and strictly subunit inner-to-outer ratio yield nested surfaces. Heliopause and shock are represented by grids and translucent projected fills. The region chooser changes emphasis and explanation. The visual exterior represents the surrounding medium, with the downstream area highlighted on selection. Grid lines are guides for depth. They are not streamlines. The model does not fit Voyager's crossing points, model a stationary shock in nature, or change its surfaces between the historical epochs.

Both trajectories and Earth's centre use the existing geometric ICRF / J2000 ecliptic Horizons vectors. Context planets use the existing [JPL orbital-element approximation](OUR-HOME-3D.md), distinct from the Earth-centre series. Planet, Sun and spacecraft glyph sizes are enlarged. The distance scale is measured at the camera focus depth. A circle marking a historical crossing can appear inside or outside the drawn surface as the surfaces are illustrative and the crossing epochs differ.

Voyager 2 crossed the shock several times. Its marker uses 30 August, the first calendar day of the published interval. The full published interval remains visible. Each marker samples the compact JPL ephemeris at **00:00 TDB** on the first published calendar day. This convention supplies a reproducible representative position. It does not convert midnight UTC or claim a measured crossing instant. Mission-phase labels use the published chronology at calendar-day resolution. The full days of single crossings and the full interval for Voyager 2's shock are displayed as transitions. If an ephemeris state is missing, the app omits a phase readout and reports the data gap. A point lying on the static schematic surface never determines its classification.

The Voyager release remains `v0.9.5-voyager-1977-2026.1` through **26 November 2026, 00:00 TDB**. Development probes requested geometric states on 1–2 January 2027 for both target IDs, but the local shell could not establish network access and the available web retrieval did not return a Horizons response. Later coverage and the reconstruction/prediction boundary could therefore not be verified. This is an access limitation and does not establish Horizons' actual stop date. Existing source names, time contract, hash pins, data gaps, 186 prior independent state comparisons and the raw archive remain as documented in [VOYAGER.md](VOYAGER.md). Horizons may distribute trajectory fits and predictions in the same file. Any future extension needs the source revision and covered intervals, consistency with the historical states and independently sampled position and velocity checks. [Horizons manual](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/manual.html) · [Vector API](https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/horizons.html).

## Implementation and checks

`heliosphere-model.mjs` constructs finite, deterministic shells and defines the event table, date resolution and AU conversion. `heliosphere-view.mjs` translates the controls and draws grids, transparent meshes and observed crossings using the existing Canvas projection and near-plane segments. The existing solar host owns navigation and the saved camera/epoch. It creates the mesh on opening, discards it on exit and shares the cached integrity-checked Voyager data and existing trails. Rendering is demand-driven and uses the existing clock suspension on tab visibility and Reduced Motion. The heliosphere adds no fetches while the layer is closed, global handlers, animation loops or dependencies.

`node --test tests/heliosphere.test.mjs` checks the shell geometry, coordinates, radii against the pinned binaries, published chronology and absent data. `npm run test:browser` includes navigation, chronology links and readouts, Reduced Motion, language, narrow layouts, restoration, lifecycle and native screenshots. Screenshots are emitted as `browser-results/heliosphere-*.png` and included in the existing Chromium Actions artifact.

Deliberate scope limits include MHD, animated solar-wind flow, a real-time boundary, calibration to both trajectories, a live IBEX/IMAP viewer and rendering the Oort Cloud. Only the local Solar System is extended.
